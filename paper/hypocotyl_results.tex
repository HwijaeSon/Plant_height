\subsection{Hypocotyl growth under a light--dark cycle}
\label{subsec:hypocotylresults}

\model{} achieved the lowest mean validation and test errors on the hypocotyl dataset collected in this study. This experiment evaluated growth at 23~$^{\circ}$C under a prescribed light--dark cycle using 943 measurements from five genotypes at seven time points. The primary evaluation comprised 35 replicate-mean targets per partition, assessing prediction of genotype-level mean growth under sparse temporal sampling and unequal replication.

Selection based on validation RMSE yielded learning rate 0.01, $\lambda_{\mathrm{ODE}}=500$, $\lambda_K=0.1$, and weight decay $10^{-4}$ for the 1,103-parameter PhytoODE model. Illumination conditions the latent vector field, while the logistic reference retains a time-independent rate and capacity for each genotype (Section~\ref{subsec:lightmodel}).

Table~\ref{tab:hypocotyl_replicate} reports training, validation, and test errors. PhytoODE achieved a test RMSE / rRMSE of $0.3460\pm0.0077$~mm / $6.85\pm0.15\%$, compared with $0.3607\pm0.0136$~mm / $7.14\pm0.27\%$ for Logistic-PINN, the strongest baseline by mean test RMSE, and $0.3679\pm0.0070$~mm / $7.28\pm0.14\%$ for the unregularized latent ODE. These values correspond to RMSE reductions of 4.07\% and 5.95\%, respectively. The improvement extends the observed performance advantage to the dataset collected in this study, with a different organ, environmental input, and sampling design.

Figure~\ref{fig:hypocotyllight} shows genotype-dependent variation in hypocotyl elongation under the shared illumination schedule. PhytoODE represents this variation through a common latent vector field and genotype embeddings, with one logistic reference per genotype. The experiment extends environmental conditioning from temperature to illumination within the same model formulation. Partition-specific means and observation masks accommodated unequal replication and missing entries without imputing lengths.

In the prespecified feature comparison, adding illumination while holding hyperparameters fixed yielded a test RMSE / rRMSE of $0.3293\pm0.0057$~mm / $6.52\pm0.11\%$, compared with $0.3646\pm0.0079$~mm / $7.22\pm0.16\%$ without illumination. This configuration attained a lower test error than the model selected by the expanded search, but a higher validation RMSE ($0.2886$ versus $0.2777$~mm). The primary table reports the configuration selected by validation performance. These results indicate predictive utility for the explicit illumination feature under the evaluated schedule, although differences in inputs and optimization budgets prevent attribution of the baseline comparison to physics regularization alone.

Secondary evaluation against individual test lengths yielded pooled RMSEs of $0.7328$~mm for PhytoODE and $0.7394$~mm for the unregularized latent ODE. These scores include within-group biological variation and use a different aggregation rule from the primary trajectory-mean metric. Evaluation across independent growth batches and additional photoperiods is required to assess generalization beyond the genotype panel and illumination schedule studied here.

The planned public release will provide the original measurements and evaluation workflow, enabling comparisons under a common sampling design and replicate-group partition. Measurement-level source identifiers, preprocessing scripts, model configurations, validation histories, and predictions will support reproducibility.

A numerical refinement check reduced the integration step from 3 to 1.5~h while holding encoder inputs fixed. The maximum change in PhytoODE predictions across the three seeds was $2.5\times10^{-6}$~mm, indicating negligible sensitivity to this refinement at the reported precision.
